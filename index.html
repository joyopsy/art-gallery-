About the artist

Surfaces that hold the light

Darshini paints with her hands, a palette knife and a trowel. Her work sits between painting and relief, where plaster ridges, hammered bowls and fragments of leaf catch a different light at every hour.

She trained in fine art and spent a decade working with architects and interior designers on large commissioned surfaces before turning to a full-time studio practice. Her series return to a few quiet subjects: the orbit, the seam, the tide and the burst of light from a single point.

Every piece is built in layers. Lime plaster is troweled, combed and left to cure, then pigment, metal leaf and found forms are set into it. No two surfaces repeat, and the finish is sealed for long life on the wall.
